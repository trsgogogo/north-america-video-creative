# North America Video Creative

Analyze a reference → extract transferable structure → match product and audience → write hooks → develop scripts and shots → create test variants → learn from actual performance. Creative methodology and adaptation notes: see [source mapping](references/source-mapping.md). Their examples and performance claims are not verified research.

## Scope

This skill consumes an existing product/audience/offer brief and produces video creative. Basic audience clarification supports the script; market entry, pricing strategy, sales operations, and full GTM planning require a separate workflow. Do not represent this skill as a complete GTM system.

Deliver scripts, storyboards and production briefs by default. A finished video requires a suitable video tool/workflow and actual rendering and inspection. Do not claim video generation, publishing, or performance testing occurred when only copy was produced. No promise of virality.

## Establish a minimum brief

Reuse known context. Identify product, buyer/job, country/language, channel, conversion action, available proof, and offer constraints. Ask only for missing information that prevents a useful or truthful deliverable. Draft with labeled assumptions when possible; never make up pricing, delivery terms, refunds, results, or inventory.

Defaults when unspecified: English copy, US-first working hypothesis with Canada requiring a separate check; concise Chinese explanation when the user speaks Chinese. North America also includes Mexico and non-English audiences: never silently treat a US English draft as validated continent-wide localization. Confirm locale when it changes the offer, research, pricing, or distribution.

## Default editorial style

- Lead with a concrete customer outcome or useful action. Use declarative titles, not questions, unless explicitly requested.
- State why buyers care before platform statistics or product specifications. Connect feature → practical benefit → evidence.
- Write natural spoken English: contractions, concrete verbs, specific scenes, short readable paragraphs. Internet fluency comes from recognition and timing, not a pile of memes.
- Humor: familiar frustration → unexpected specific comparison → useful resolution. Aim at the situation or your own process. Offer a clean alternative when the joke distracts from buying intent.
- Avoid generic AI prose, empty superlatives, “game-changing,” “unlock your potential,” fake urgency, and “everyone needs this.” Do not invent a founder's personal story.
- Study another creator's structure and persuasion sequence; write original language and examples. Attribute factual observations and borrowed frameworks. Do not reproduce distinctive copy wholesale.
- Treat “#1,” “best,” percentages, savings, and “we tested” as claims requiring support. A customer benefit may be a goal, a demonstrated capability, or an observed result: make the distinction explicit.

## Evidence before amplification

Maintain known facts, hypotheses, and missing evidence separately. Source documents teach structures; their performance numbers, regulations, studies, technologies, and case results are unverified and must not enter customer copy as facts.

Use actual quotes and links for customer research; synthetic personas are hypotheses, not interviews. Verify current platform availability, ad formats, prices, policies, trends, and consequential legal/medical/financial assertions with appropriate primary sources when those details matter. If verification is unavailable, say so and draft around supported facts.

Never manufacture testimonials, expert credentials, screenshots, results, scarcity, endorsements, reviews, demand, or benchmark runs. Label dramatizations and mockups; do not present generated images as product proof. Do not frame unsupported fear or body shame as a customer need. Replace those tactics with a specific problem, real demonstration, and proportionate next step.

Discounts, returns, guarantees, eligibility and fulfillment promises must come from actual terms. Omit absent terms from public copy; put needed inputs in a separate production note. For regulated claims or promotions, identify the relevant locale and verify applicable requirements before making compliance claims.

## Work at the right scope

Generate actual copy, not just a template, unless the user asks for a template. Match CTA to the buyer's next step: save a useful checklist, inspect a demo, request a quote, start a trial, or buy. Choose one primary CTA per asset.

Use the user's chosen channel; adapt pacing, aspect ratio and CTA as a production brief, verifying current technical requirements when necessary. Do not automatically publish, launch ads, send messages, or spend money. Pass creative feedback using creative-iteration.md without implying separate agents were launched.

## Finish and report

Check: audience specificity; headline/body match; proof for material claims; natural English; requested scope; one useful CTA; feasible execution; and a measurable next step where appropriate. Revise weak copy before delivering.

For substantial deliverables add a brief receipt: skill used, references actually read, research/tools actually performed, files created, and remaining assumptions. For headline-only requests keep it to a short note outside the copy. Never say an integration, campaign, test, or benchmark ran when only a plan was produced.
