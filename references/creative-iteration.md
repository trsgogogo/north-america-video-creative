# Creative testing and feedback

## Diagnose the available evidence

Identify asset ID, version, platform, date window, distribution (paid/organic/unknown), audience, duration, spend where relevant, and metric definitions. Distinguish observed metrics from creative judgment. Record denominators and attribution windows; comparisons across platforms or video lengths may be misleading.

- Early drop-off: inspect whether the first frame and opening communicate relevance. Hypothesis, not proof of cause.
- Mid-video drop-off: inspect repeated information, delayed payoff, unclear demo and pacing.
- Views without action: inspect audience fit, promise/CTA match, evidence and next-step friction.
- Clicks without purchase: flag possible destination/offer/qualification issues for the owner; do not automatically blame the script.

If only a script is available, give an editorial diagnosis and test plan, not a performance report.

## Produce variants worth testing

Create distinct hypotheses, not synonym swaps. Vary a hook family, first-frame demonstration, proof placement, or CTA friction. Keep body, audience, offer and distribution comparable when isolating an opening. When several elements change, label the test exploratory.

Experiment table: asset/version | hypothesis | changed element | controlled elements | primary metric | downstream guardrail | exposure/spend cap | observation window | decision rule | owner.

No universal winning CTR, retention rate, sample size or spend: use actual baseline and constraints. With limited observations, state uncertainty and treat results as directional. Organic distribution is not randomized evidence of causal lift. Calculate rates with explicit denominators; zero denominator means unavailable. High watch time with poor qualified action is not automatically a winning sales creative.

## Close the feedback loop

Creator → editor: script, intended payoff, required footage, proof and pacing.
Editor → reviewer: rendered version, intentional changes, missing assets, actual duration.
Analytics/sales → creator: measured drop-off, repeated buyer objections, qualified responses and sources.
Product/operations → creator: actual capabilities, availability and fulfillment limits that constrain claims.

Track: asset/version | observation and source | interpretation | next edit | owner | due date | check/result. Close a feedback item when its change and review are recorded. These are workflow roles, not automatic subagents or authorization to send external messages.

Final deliverable: revised creative, explanation of material changes, next test and unresolved evidence gaps. Publishing and paid tests require the actual user authorization and available execution tools; do not schedule recurring work just because this SOP mentions iteration.
