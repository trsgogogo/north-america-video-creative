# 36 hook families for North American short videos

These are creative options, not empirically ranked winners. Examples are original draft angles, not proven customer outcomes. A numeral describing the number of steps requires the body to contain that many steps; a numeral describing performance requires evidence.

Use one primary mechanism and at most one supporting mechanism. Default: 10 distinct hooks across suitable families, then shortlist three with a short reason. Judge relevance, clarity, credible payoff, distinctiveness, and channel fit as editorial criteria, not predicted conversion rates.

## Headlines and humor

Lead with the buyer's useful payoff. Weak: “A platform reported 24% more conversions.” Better as a non-claim instructional headline: “Find the Ads That Bring Buyers Back.” Use “We cut acquisition cost by 24%” only if an actual, comparable measurement supports it.

Three tonal variants for an educational capacity brief:
- Clear: “A Better Brief for Your Next GPU Quote.”
- Dry humor: “Your AI Demo Works. Now Meet Procurement.”
- Direct: “Put Workload, Region, and Timeline in the First Message.”

Humor should survive without a trending meme. Do not sprinkle “bro,” “cooked,” or “POV” into every asset. Use customer language collected from real sources when available. Keep curiosity promises payable within the asset; do not withhold the answer solely to force engagement.
